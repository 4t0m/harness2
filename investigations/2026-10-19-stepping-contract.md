## Competing hypotheses and discriminating tests

1. **Contract/schema ambiguity.** The model may output literal schema notation such as `step_left|step_right`, or an older dance name, because the system/schema does not clearly require one enum value. Check exact emitted move strings in every saved `raw_response.message.content`, and compare the stepping `SYSTEM` and `SCHEMA` to the prior phrase runner's concrete enum and wording. Literal pipe strings, especially across cases, support this; valid exact IDs in the raw text rule it out as the direct cause.
2. **Runner/parser mismatch.** The runner may extract the wrong content field or pass transformed text to `compile_steps`, or the compiler may reject an actually legal ID. Compare raw native response content, recorded `content`, and recorded error for each call. Feed exact saved content to the frozen `parse_steps` as a read-only control, and feed a hand-written legal plan to the same parser. Disagreement between recorded and independent results supports this; exact agreement rules it out.
3. **Model execution failure despite a clear contract.** The model may ignore a correct, concrete enum after receiving it. Verify the actual messages and response schema in saved call receipts, then count legal/illegal emitted identifiers by model and case. A concrete enum and correct runner path together with repeated illegal IDs supports this; a pipe-valued schema or prompt points back to hypothesis 1.
4. **Source drift or mixed evidence.** The compiler or runner could have changed during the live run, making recorded errors incomparable to current code. Recompute every saved source SHA-256 against the current files and inspect the evidence `complete` flag and call counts before concluding. Hash mismatch supports drift; matching hashes rules out current-source drift, though not unsaved runtime state.

All eight native responses report `done_reason: stop` and contain parseable JSON with exactly the `moves` top-level key. There is no detected source drift.

**Exact emitted identifiers.** E2B failed 4/4. Its first two replies use `0.20m left|0.0m right`, `0.40m left|0.0m right`, etc. as move IDs, with `soft|clear` as the variation. Its third uses coordinate-like strings such as `0.20m left|0.0`; its fourth uses `0.20m|0.00m`. Qwen failed 3/4: its invalid move IDs are `step_left|step_right` or `step_right|step_left`, and the last reply also uses `clear|soft` variations. Qwen's `two_left_then_two_right` reply uses exact legal IDs and variations and compiled successfully. Thus the failure is not universal to this model/schema path.

**Parser/runner control.** For every call, saved native `raw_response.message.content` equals the recorded `content`. Re-running frozen `parse_steps` on each exact saved content gives seven `ValueError: unknown step move` and one pass, identical to the recorded outcomes. A hand-written four-step JSON plan with only `step_left`/`step_right` and `soft`/`clear` also passes. This rules out a runner extraction mismatch or parser rejection of legal strings as the direct cause. It does not establish why models selected illegal strings.

**Prompt/schema comparison.** The stepping public contract embeds literal pipe alternatives inside its JSON example: `"step_left|step_right"` and `"soft|clear"`. The stepping JSON schema constrains array shape but gives each item only `type: string`; it has no identifier enum. The prior phrase runner also used `"soft|clear"` in prose/example but supplied a concrete enum union of legal move and variation strings in its schema, and its cases compiled. This difference makes contract/schema interaction a strong explanation for the copied pipe values. It is not proof that schema alone caused the failures: E2B invented coordinate-valued move strings, and Qwen generated one legal plan despite the same stepping schema. The locally changed interaction is the new step contract, example, and permissive schema, not a demonstrated upstream model change.

## Assessment

The direct cause of the seven compiler failures is invalid model-emitted identifiers, with exact raw/recorded/parser agreement and matching frozen source hashes. Confidence is high on that mechanism. Confidence is moderate that the pipe-valued example plus permissive schema contributed to it; model noncompliance remains a competing contributor. The baseline measures this first-pass interface as shipped: E2B 0/4 compiled, Qwen 1/4 compiled. It does not measure whether either model can plan side steps under a clearer contract.

## Bounded diagnostic design and correction proposal

The proposed 2×2 prompt × schema diagnostic is reasonable for these **same, now-observed four cases**. Reuse the eight original-prompt/free-schema baseline calls without rerunning them. Make three new conditions, four cases × two models each (24 calls): (1) explicit legal identifiers and no pipe example with the original free-string schema; (2) original prompt with an enum-union schema; (3) explicit identifiers with enum-union schema. Rotate condition order within each model; preserve every raw response, exact prompt/schema, native completion status and timing, compiler acceptance, and separate host semantic correctness. Keep the compiler and fixtures frozen. Treat improvements as interface diagnosis on reused cases, not held-out gains. The enum union constrains legal strings but not their pair positions; the compiler remains the final typed gate. Avoid replacing the pipe example with one complete answer that primes a particular four-step order; use prose listing the two exact move IDs and two exact variations plus the schema for structure.

If a repair comparison is still useful after that diagnostic, pair two **single-call** conditions on each originally failed model/case: a correction arm includes the exact previous assistant reply followed by structural feedback naming only the legal IDs/variations and the compiler error, while a fresh arm receives the same clarified contract/schema and original request without the previous reply. Use identical decoding settings, at most one response per arm, randomized arm order, and no expected move sequence or final-position oracle in either prompt. Score strict compilation and semantic correctness separately. This would test whether feedback/prior reply adds value beyond a clear fresh interface; it would still be a reused-case repair result, not generalization. This delegated investigation made no correction calls, code edits, or normalization; the parent retains the user’s broader authorization for subsequent experiments.

## Procedural motion limits observed in the frozen output

The compiled step is kinematically contact-aware, but its root follows the **average of ankle x positions**, not a center-of-mass or support-polygon calculation. In a right step at local frame 5, only the left foot is marked planted at x = -0.18 m, while the pelvis is at x = +0.05 m. This does not itself prove a fall (the pelvis is not the center of mass), but the validator cannot establish balance. The moving foot's height uses a sine on each half-step: it is grounded at frame 0, rises 0.02163 m by frame 1 (0.05 s later), and is grounded again at frame 10 after an equal final height change. Thus the sampled vertical speed near lift/landing is about 0.433 m/s, and the sine has nonzero endpoint vertical derivative despite smooth horizontal interpolation. Contact flags switch at those samples; there is no impact/force continuity validation. Segment length, planted-foot sliding, finite geometry, and planned root bounds are checked, but dynamic balance, collisions, physical contact forces, and dance quality remain outside this prototype's evidence.
